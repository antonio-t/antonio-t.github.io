When graphic designers are entrusted with a creative production task, they explore stock image databases in order to find images suitable for the design. These images are normally listed according to the relevance to the query, which makes similar images cluster together. This kind of interface is a bottleneck for the creative process of designers, who find themselves scrolling down the long image list trying to figure out which image matches best their needs. Instead, designers would ideally like to understand at first sight the most representative image patterns and be able to narrow them down to the specific image of their preference, in a coarse-to-fine manner. In this paper, we propose an exploration method that allows a comprehensive visualization of the search results by controlling the amount of diversity of the images displayed. Specifically, by dividing the image exploration process into multiple steps from maximum diversity to minimum (i.e., maximum similarity), we manage to emulate the aforementioned coarse-to-fine image exploration, supporting designers in their creative process. We also propose an experimental design for our method, which we plan to evaluate with the cooperation of designers (work in progress).
